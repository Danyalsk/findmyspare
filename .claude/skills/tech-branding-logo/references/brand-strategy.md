# Brand strategy

## Minimum input

Capture: product and maturity; primary user; painful situation; alternatives; job-to-be-done; product promise; proof/mechanism; category; business model if relevant; expansion path; competitors; desired user feeling before/during/after use; must-feel and must-not-feel traits; parent/sub-brand relationship; and launch touchpoints.

## Psychology model

Write one sentence each for: user transformation, brand role, core promise, reasons to believe, personality tensions, behavioral principles, anti-positioning, emotional benefit, and visual territories. Prefer tensions such as “technical but not cold” over adjective piles. Tie every strategic claim to product evidence or label it as a hypothesis.

## Mode gates

- **Zero-to-brand:** do not name or design until the foundation and naming territories are coherent. For immature hypotheses, recommend a provisional identity scope.
- **Name-to-brand:** assess literal meaning, associations, pronunciation, memory, flexibility, competitor proximity, and emotional fit. Do not invent mythology to justify a weak name.
- **Brand evolution:** establish what current identity communicates, what users recognize, what has equity, and what the product has outgrown before proposing new aesthetics.

## Positioning sentence

Use: “For [primary user] who [undesirable situation], [product] is the [category/role] that [unique outcome], because [proof]. Unlike [alternative], it [meaningful distinction].” Keep the final sentence concise and test it against real product or service behavior.
