# Quality control

Score each 1–5 and attach evidence: originality/differentiation, simplicity, memorability, silhouette, small-size function, monochrome function, legibility, scalability, product fit, emotional fit, system extensibility, accessibility/contrast, production readiness, and migration safety for rebrands.

## Scope-aware evidence

Select relevant criteria and record `not_applicable` with rationale for others; exclude these from averages. Identity checks use intended logo surfaces. Campaign checks cover objective/audience fit, key visual and channel adaptation, product fidelity, exact copy/claims, attribution, fixed requirements and permitted overrides. Application checks cover source-brand fidelity, requested deliverables and production constraints. Verify master identity files remain unchanged for temporary work.

Record test dimensions/surface, observed result and artifact path. A conceptual preview can pass concept review while production remains `hold`. Do not claim a physical print/stitch test from a screen approximation. Visual improvement requires matched evaluation, not only a self-assigned score.

## Gates

- Any applicable blocker in legibility, intended-size function, accessibility, brand continuity, required factual fidelity, or production readiness means `iterate` regardless of average.
- A candidate must average at least 3.5/5 to enter developed directions.
- The selected direction should average at least 4/5, with no score below 3, unless the user explicitly accepts the risk.
- “Looks modern” is not evidence. Cite the tested size, surface, comparison, or construction observation.

## Common failures

Generic tech shorthand; over-detailed marks; thin strokes; trapped counters; color-dependent recognition; name mythology with no product truth; three near-identical directions; trendy type that harms utility; a rebrand that discards equity without diagnosis; and guidelines that describe a logo but do not govern the product experience.
