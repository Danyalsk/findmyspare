# Lasting identity workflow

Use only for `brand-identity`. Scale exploration to the requested scope; do not restart approved stages. Wordmarks, custom lettering, monograms, emblems, illustrative identities and symbols are equal candidates when supported by the brief.


1. **Brief the product.** Capture audience, job-to-be-done, alternatives, promise, proof, product maturity, category, constraints, expansion path, desired feelings, anti-feelings, and touchpoints. Mark unknowns.
2. **Form the brand foundation.** Define transformation, brand role, core promise, reasons to believe, personality tensions, attributes, anti-positioning, verbal tone, and 3–5 visual territories. Do not proceed if the foundation is contradictory; surface the conflict.
3. **Explore subjects when useful.** For symbol-led exploration, produce 15–25 concrete nouns across objects, structures, movements, nature, people/roles, and cultural or technical metaphors. Translate abstractions into subjects. Narrow to about 15 approved nouns with rationale. Avoid automatic clouds, brains, sparks, circuits, shields, robots, generic gradients, or letter-in-circle marks unless the concept earns them.
4. **Generate quantity before polish.** For an exploratory symbol-led identity, produce at least 50 low-cost thumbnail directions. For lettering, wordmarks, emblems or tightly constrained work, choose an appropriate exploration breadth and record why; nouns are an optional ideation tool, not a requirement to invent a symbol. For non-sketching agents, each is a structured concept: noun(s), silhouette, geometry, positive/negative space, story, intended-application potential, originality risk, and small-size risk.
5. **Combine and select.** For symbol-led work seek unexpected noun combinations using overlap, negative space, rhythm, flow, modularity, or a meaningful twist. For a full identity exploration, score and shortlist 8–15 concepts and develop 3 distinct directions. For type-led or constrained work, document selection criteria and breadth appropriate to the brief. Do not show three color variations of one idea as three directions.
6. **Refine in black and white first.** Establish silhouette, proportion, spacing, optical balance, line weight, negative space, and construction. Then create wordmark/typography, color, and lockups. Read `references/logo-workflow.md`.
7. **Test as a product asset.** Choose tests from the brief and domain module: digital icons and UI for technology; menus, labels and packaging for food; garment reproduction for apparel; distance signage and listings for real estate. Check relevant lockups, monochrome and actual minimum reproduction sizes. A favicon is required only when it is a deliverable. Reject marks that depend on fine lines, tight gaps, or color alone.
8. **Extend the visual DNA.** Extend the identity’s type, composition, imagery, materials and, where relevant, geometry into the requested surfaces: packaging, signage, garments, menus, UI, documentation, motion or social assets. Include only modules that fit the product; badges are optional.
9. **Package and explain.** Use `references/deliverable-schemas.md` for the final manifest. Include rationale, alternatives rejected, usage rules, risks, and open decisions. For rebrands include migration sequencing and continuity measures.
10. **Run QC.** Use `references/qc.md`; show scores, evidence, and unresolved risks. A score is a decision aid, not proof of market success.
